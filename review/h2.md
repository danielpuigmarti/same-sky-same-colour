# H2: how we know it is carbon dioxide

Open `h2.png`. Left: the comet's brightness against colour on both nights, with the two neighbouring photos ringed
and the August 2025 photos of the same places in grey. Right: for each of the photos inside the band, the photo,
the August photo of the same sky at the same colour, and their difference. Under the chart: the headline and its
checks.

**Number to read**

- Contrast: 15.6 ± 1.4 on 26 February and 9.7 ± 0.9 on 25 February, with an aperture of 12.3 arcsec radius and a ring of 40 to 55 pixels.

**It passes if**

1. The contrast is 5 or more on both nights, and stays 5 or more when any one photo that is not a night's brightest
   is left out (lowest: 6.7).
2. The page says what follows, or you decide that it need not: with a night's brightest photo left out, the next
   brightest gives 25 February 3.1; 26 February 8.1. On 25 February the other photo inside the band sits at its edge, so
   that night's figure rests on the brightest.
3. The headline is quoted with its aperture (12.3 arcsec radius) and its ring (40 to 55 pixels), and the fluxes are called lower limits. The contrast itself is
   not a limit: with the nearer ring it is 16.7 and 10.2.
4. Every August template is under 5% of its comet flux (largest: 2.9%), and every photo
   inside the band other than a night's brightest is above its template by 5 standard errors or more (smallest: 5.3).
5. The word "only" is nowhere on the sheet, and "first" and "discovery" are nowhere in the comet's captions.
