4 photos of 26 February 2026 taken about 2 minutes apart, each with its time (UTC), the wavelength at
the comet and the flux in the aperture. Under the tiles: the display rule and the contrast.

**Number to read**

- Contrast on 26 February: 263.2 / mean(14.1, 19.5) = 15.6 ± 1.4. The neighbour above the band (4.384 µm) was taken on 25 February and is not among the tiles.

**It passes if**

1. Every tile shows the same pixels of sky at the same display rule: the stars sit in the same places from tile to
   tile, and the rule is printed once for all.
2. The wavelengths printed are those of `site/data/lemmon/spectrum.json` (4.1929, 4.2291, 4.2656, 4.3023 µm; August 4.2734 µm).
3. The contrast is 5 or more.
4. The August tile is empty at the mark: its flux (+0.5 mJy) is under 5% of the comet's (263.2 mJy).
5. No star is hidden: a pixel flagged unusable is drawn with the median of its usable neighbours, nothing else is
   changed, and the marks leave the middle free.
6. What the caption says about the 4.19 µm tile matches what is visible there: the comet is faint but
   present (14.1 mJy).
