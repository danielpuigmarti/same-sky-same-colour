How the pictures are drawn
  • Each square is 6.15 arcsec across on the sky, the size of one pixel of the telescope's camera, and holds the value of one pixel of a photo. North is up and east is to the left in every picture, so that two visits line up.
  • Nothing is smoothed, unless you switch on Smooth pixels. That changes how the screen enlarges the picture and none of its values: the readout under the pointer says the same.
  • When several photos of a visit are shown together, each pixel is the mean of the photos that could be used there, each without its own sky background. A pixel is shown when at least half of the photos of each visit could be used; elsewhere there is no data.
  • The two pictures share one brightness rule. Black and white are set from the pixels of both together (white at the 99.5th percentile), with one fixed curve between them, so a value has the same grey in both.
  • Blink changes picture at most 2 times a second. The space bar plays and pauses, the arrow keys step, and D shows the difference.
  • Hatching marks pixels that are saturated or damaged. A veil covers pixels where the two visits were not taken at the same colour. Small ticks beside a place mark where a known fast star should be on the date of the picture.
  • The difference is drawn in units of the noise, from −5 to +5. Anything smaller than 3 times the noise is grey. It is the only picture that is resampled.
